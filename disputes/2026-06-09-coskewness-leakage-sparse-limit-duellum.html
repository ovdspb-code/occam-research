When Triple Correlations Fall Silent

Could the rare, near-simultaneous co-firing of three neurons quietly hijack a pairwise model's "explained entropy" until the number means nothing? One side argued that in the sparse limit it must. The other showed the yardstick is built from quantities that triple correlations cannot touch.

Neuroscience increasingly reads the brain through the joint statistics of many neurons firing at once. A favourite tool is the pairwise maximum-entropy model: the simplest distribution that reproduces how often each neuron fires and how often each pair fires together, while assuming nothing more. How much of a population's structure that pairwise model captures is summarised by its explained entropy — and a long literature treats a high value as evidence that pairwise interactions are the real mechanism.

The toolkit in one breath. Take many neurons, each either silent (0) or spiking (1) in a time bin. The pairwise maximum-entropy model matches only the single-neuron firing rates and the pairwise co-firing rates, then spreads its uncertainty as evenly as possible. Explained entropy, written S₁−S₂, measures how much of the population's total dependency that pairwise model accounts for. The rival quantity is co-skewness — a third-order statistic asking whether triplets of neurons spike together in a lopsided way. The seed paper warned that such higher-order structure can "leak" into a fitted model and be mistaken for a first-order effect.

The seed paper showed that when a model matches only low-order statistics, missing higher-order structure can be absorbed into the fitted parameters — a "feature leakage" with, for sparse binary data, a co-skewness signature. This duel did not dispute that leakage exists. It interrogated a far more ambitious, riskier idea the paper never claimed: that in the sparse limit the leakage does not merely exist but takes over.

The frozen claim

The thesis, fixed before the duel and not edited afterwards (translated from the registered Russian text):

"In a biologically realistic sparse regime, co-skewness-driven leakage does not merely appear but dominates asymptotically: as p → 0 (mean activity toward zero) at fixed pairwise correlation structure, the entropy fraction wrongly attributed to first-order parameters rises monotonically toward 1, so that a pairwise maximum-entropy model's 'explained entropy' converges to a quantity set entirely by higher-order co-skewness rather than a genuine pairwise mechanism — hence in the sparse limit this measure is asymptotically uninformative about mechanism."

The falsifier both sides accepted: any sparse correlated-binary family in which, as p → 0, the leakage fraction stays bounded below by the true pairwise contribution — or shrinks — refutes the claimed dominance. One explicit counterexample is enough.

Thesis · Aramis
In the sparse limit, triple correlations take over. As firing grows rare, the share of entropy a pairwise model claims as "explained" is really higher-order leakage in disguise; the measure converges to something set by co-skewness, not by any genuine pairwise mechanism. The yardstick goes blind.
Counter · Atos
Explained entropy is built only from first and second moments — the very quantities the thesis holds fixed. Co-skewness moves the leftover, unexplained entropy, not the explained part. And in the standard sparse model, the triple term fades faster than the pairwise one, not slower.

The duel: arithmetic, not rhetoric

Each contested step was a small distribution with a known answer, so the two sides could compute who was right rather than argue it. Three things got settled.

Cleared away: the first "leakage → 1" was a numerical artifact

The thesis's opening demonstration looked decisive — at p = 0.05 a "leakage fraction" raced toward 1. But the counter-side traced it to a degeneracy: the pairwise model had collapsed to a near-zero-entropy state, and the leakage fraction was being read off a ratio of two near-zeros. A 0/0 reported as "dominance" is optimiser breakdown, not a scaling law.

The counterexample: a dichotomized Gaussian where leakage shrinks

The counter-side then built the falsifier the thesis had invited — an equicorrelated dichotomized Gaussian, the standard latent-variable model for correlated spikes. By a classical result of Sibuya (1960) on the asymptotic independence of Gaussian tails, its triple correlation decays exponentially faster than its pairwise one as activity grows rare. Computed exactly, the higher-order fraction of the population's dependency does not climb toward 1 — it stays under six percent all the way down to a mean firing rate of one spike in two hundred bins (Fig. 2). That is a family where leakage falls, which is exactly what the falsifier required.

The decisive point: the yardstick is made of the wrong moments

The thesis defender replied that the dichotomized Gaussian is only one family, and that real ensembles with a shared latent drive (attention, arousal) might carry genuine tail dependence. Fair — but it does not reach the quantity under dispute. Explained entropy, S₁−S₂, is a function of the first and second moments alone, and the thesis holds both fixed. Co-skewness can move the unexplained residual S₂−SN and bias the fitted couplings, but it cannot make the explained part higher-order. A direct test confirms it: sweep the triple moment across its whole feasible range, sign change included, with means and pairwise correlations pinned — and the explained entropy does not budge to machine precision, while the true entropy swings by 0.18 bits (Fig. 1).

The verdict

Outcome (who argued better): CON — the refuting side prevailed on argumentation.

Substantive verdict (was the claim true): con — the universal sparse-limit dominance does not hold.

The two axes agree here. The side that argued better is also the side the merits favour: the thesis's claim of universal co-skewness dominance in the sparse limit was refuted, not merely out-argued.

The arbiter judged the refuting side (Atos) closer to correct: it caught the 0/0 artifact in the thesis's first calculation, supplied a working dichotomized-Gaussian counterexample where the triple term fades faster than the pairwise one, and held the dispute to the stated quantity — explained entropy — where the decisive definitional argument applied. The claimed universal limit for the entropy fraction did not survive. What remains open is narrower: specific sparse families with particular bias and coupling structure could still behave differently, and that is a question about parameters, not about the asserted limit.

What this supports

  • A pairwise model's explained entropy S₁−S₂ is fixed by the first and second moments alone — so co-skewness cannot make it asymptotically higher-order while those moments are held fixed.
  • In the standard dichotomized-Gaussian sparse model, higher-order correlations decay faster than pairwise ones as p → 0; the captured fraction stays above 94% down to p ≈ 0.005.
  • Numerical "near-zero over near-zero" ratios demand a stability check before they are read as scaling laws.

What this does not support

  • It does not survey every sparse family. Exotic models with strong shared-gain tail dependence are untested here and could move the residual differently.
  • It does not deny that leakage exists or that it can bias fitted couplings Jij — only that it cannot capture the explained-entropy yardstick in the stated limit.
  • It does not measure real neural data. The tests are exact but synthetic; they establish possibility and a definitional bound, not actuality.

Where the losing side still has a point

The thesis defender's strongest residual point is fair: "co-skewness-driven leakage" was never given a sharp definition, and the dispute settled the explained-entropy reading rather than every conceivable sense of the phrase. A shared latent drive can, in principle, produce genuine tail dependence that this dichotomized-Gaussian family does not contain; whether any biologically realistic ensemble actually lives in such a regime is a real, unresolved empirical question — just not one that touches the fixed-moment yardstick the thesis named. That is the honest next experiment, and a synthetic world cannot settle it.

Prior-work note

This release makes no originality claim. The seed paper established that feature leakage exists and, for sparse correlated binary inputs, takes a co-skewness form, and proposed diagnostics to separate prediction from mechanism. The duel's contribution is a stress-test of a much stronger scaling claim the paper did not make. Novelty status: not an originality claim.
